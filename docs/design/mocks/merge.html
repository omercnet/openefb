Fable's spine, Opus's reading order. כנף is the wing: the ownship glyph on the chart and the marker on the active leg's gauge are the same shape.

From FableThe skeleton: the flight is a nav log. One strip pinned to the chart's edge (right edge in Hebrew), in three tenses, Next, Now and Flown, is also the plan, the debrief and the logbook. No tabs, floating buttons, bottom strip or sheets.

From OpusThe reading order: future is loud. The next thing ahead tops the strip in the biggest type, rows run in time order led by their time-to, and the track is ruled in the same minutes, so a row and a tick are one fact.

In flight, iPad, day

Near KPAO, 2,450 ft, 98 kt, TRK 312, +300 fpm, KOAK 18.4 NM.STAGE 2
The idea

The strip reads top to bottom: what is next, what is now, what is done. The amber row is a predicted event in the log, between KPAO and KOAK, with its assumption printed under it. Its time, 1:50, is the diamond on the chart's track, between the 1 and 2 minute ticks; 6:12 is the second diamond. Waypoints and events share one row: glyph, time-to, what, detail.

Replaces

The bottom instrument strip (readouts now live in the active leg cell), the six floating circles (now four 60pt cells in the footer: layer, night, zoom in, zoom out), the NRST box (a footer cell that inserts a direct-to row) and the corridor column from Opus (folded into the tape). When the fix goes stale the active cell greys, predictions switch off and the GPS cell says GPS LOST 14 S in red.

B

In flight, iPad, day

The same moment without a prediction engine.STAGE 1
The idea

What ships first: the next thing is the next waypoint, KOAK in 11:16 at the top in the biggest type, on a white row because it is not a caution. The chart is still ruled in minutes (groundspeed times time, no airspace logic), and the tape shows altitude and climb only.

Replaces

Nothing extra: stage 2 adds rows to this same strip and diamonds to this same track. No screen changes shape between stages, which is why the skeleton can ship before the engine.

C

In flight, iPad, night

Same as A, night palette.STAGE 2
The idea

No filled amber block at night: the next-thing row becomes an amber rule on the left edge and amber type on near-black, the same meaning at about a tenth of the light. The chart is near black and desaturated, the route and airspace keep their hue at low luminance, the paper is #0D1013 with warm grey ink.

Replaces

The night toggle button: NIGHT is a footer cell that shows its state (AUTO from sun elevation) and overrides on tap.

D

Home: the log stack

Tomorrow's plan, today's flight live, three flown with per-leg grades, totals.STAGE 2 ROW
The idea

Home is the logbook and the flight list at once: every flight is a strip in the same grammar, stacked under Next, Now and Flown. Each strip leads with its loudest fact (time to departure, the next event, the block time). Grades sit on the legs they belong to, and an amber leg stays amber here. Tapping a strip makes it the spine and gives the chart the rest of the screen.

Replaces

The tab bar, Flights > History / Logbook / Plans, and the separate logbook list. The totals footer is the logbook's summary. In the live strip, the diamonds on the active leg are the same events as in A.

E

Airport, opened in the strip column

KOAK tapped in flight. Chart stays live.STAGE 1
The idea

An airport is a row opened up. The column keeps its width and its footer, so GPS state, NRST and zoom never leave the screen. The first line after the name is weather with its age; frequencies have a label column; runways are a list beside a small diagram. Direct KOAK inserts the row; Log returns the strip.

Replaces

The airport bottom sheet that covered the chart. Here the chart keeps 66% of the width and rings KOAK, so the pilot can still see where it is.

F

Flown: CVFR debrief

LLHZ to LLIB via TAVOR and DESHE.STAGE 1
The idea

Left: the flight's own strip with the actuals written in, a grade per leg at readout size and the reason under it. Right: the same line unrolled, three bands on one distance axis: where you were across the route, where you were in height against the ±200 ft band, and what was said at each point. Leg 1 is at half scale with a marked break so the short legs where things happened get the room.

Replaces

The debrief table and any overall score: 96, 71 and 93 stay separate, the 71 is amber with its reason, and DESHE says "not heard" (a transcript exists) rather than "not checked". Aerodrome zones are hatched and labelled not scored.

Same moment as A.STAGE 2
The idea

The strip folds to a 104pt rail on the left edge: the same rows in the same order, the next thing still the biggest type on the screen. The active leg cell moves to the bottom at full width with the readouts at 38pt and the tape, then one footer row of 60pt cells within thumb reach.

Replaces

The compact layout that stacked a HUD, an instrument strip and floating buttons. Layout switches on size class, not device: an iPad in Split View gets this screen.

Hebrew

In a Hebrew UI the rail and the iPad strip mirror to the right edge; the chart does not mirror. Times and idents stay left-to-right inside right-to-left rows.
