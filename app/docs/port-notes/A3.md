## Swift behaviour kept (quirks, not fixed)

- `process` lower-cases everything first, so recogniser capitals are lost.
- Most converted phrases swallow the whitespace after them, because their digit group ends in `\s*`: "squawk one two zero
  zero and ident" gives `Squawk 1200and ident`, "heading three six zero then descend" gives `heading 360then descend`. A runway
  that ends in left, right or center does not ("runway two seven left squawk ..." gives `Runway 27L Squawk 1200...`).
- The digit pattern lists `nine` before `niner`, and a regex alternation takes the first alternative that lets the pattern
  match, so wherever a pattern can end right after "nine" the word "niner" becomes `9` plus a stray `r`: "squawk one two zero
  niner" gives `Squawk 1209r`, "runway niner" gives `Runway 9r`. Only the frequency pattern, which needs whitespace after each
  whole-number digit, reads "niner". `AviationVocabularyTests.ninerToNine` passes because it only checks for "1209". Pinned in
  `aviationVocabulary.test.ts` ("niner leaves a stray r").
- An N-number takes at most 6 words after "november"; "at" matches inside words ("that five" gives `that 5`); altitudes
  are always written with commas here (Swift used the device locale's number formatter).
- SpeechAnalyzer got only the region terms as contextual strings; the 24 radio terms went to `SFSpeechRecognizer`. iOS here is
  SpeechAnalyzer only, so the radio terms are used on Android only. Passing them to SpeechAnalyzer too is one line in
  `speechAnalyzer.ts` if S2 measures a gain.
- A flight whose engine could not start keeps its flight context until `stopTranscription` (the Swift phase tests rely on it).
- The last unfinished utterance is dropped at stop.
- Audio or transcription failure never stops the recording (coordinator behaviour, L4).

## Differences from Swift

- **Own audio capture.** Swift's `AudioRecorder` fed the service PCM buffers stamped with the file position. expo-audio gives
  JavaScript no PCM, so the iOS module runs its own AVAudioEngine tap, and the recorder and the analyzer capture the
  microphone side by side (A2 device check 5). Segment times are seconds of audio delivered to the analyzer, and
  `TranscriptionSession` places them on the flight timeline by wall clock: offset = time between `startTranscription` being
  called and the engine being ready (the coordinator starts the recorder first, so file time 0 is just before that call).
  Expect a drift against the audio file of about the recorder's start latency, plus a little after an interruption (see
  below). A segment time is the end of the audio the analyzer had heard, not a file position.
- **Interruptions.** The iOS module keeps its analyzer session across a phone call or Siri: when the system interruption
  ends with `shouldResume`, on a route change (`AVAudioEngineConfigurationChange`) and when the app becomes active with the
  engine stopped, it restarts the engine and carries on. Frames are contiguous, so transcript time skips the gap the way the
  file does. The gap marker row (`[INTERRUPTION: phoneCall]`) is the coordinator's job on the recorder's gap event, unchanged.
  If the engine cannot restart, the module reports a failure and the session retries (every analyzer failure is retried the
  same way, then given up on).
- **Engine ends and errors** (new, Swift had none: it logged and stayed "transcribing"). An engine that ends by itself is
  started again after 250 ms; one that ends within 1 s of starting having heard nothing counts as failing. Failures back off
  500 ms, 1 s, ... 8 s, reset on a result or after 30 s of running, and the sixth failure in a row makes transcription give up,
  release the engine and call `setOnError` with `transcriptionUnavailable`. A refused permission or missing recogniser gives up at once.
  A restart is skipped when a stop or a newer start came first; start and stop run one at a time in call order.
  `stopTranscription` therefore waits for a start still in flight, including one waiting on a system permission dialog.
- **iOS has no `SFSpeechRecognizer` fallback.** Swift fell back to it when `SpeechTranscriber.installedLocales` lacked en-US.
  The module reports `assetsMissing` (start never downloads, so a flight never waits on the network) and `start` fails with
  `transcriptionUnavailable`, the flight records without a transcript. `prepareSpeechAssets()` installs the model; **I1 should
  call it at launch** (a no-op when installed). A device that cannot run SpeechTranscriber (`SpeechTranscriber.isAvailable`)
  and iOS before 26 report the same.
- **Android** (Swift had no Android): expo-speech-recognition with `requiresOnDeviceRecognition`. The module binds a recogniser
  to the device only on Android 13 and later; below that it would use the default recogniser, which on many phones is Google's
  cloud service and would send cockpit audio off the phone. So the engine is unavailable on Android 12 and earlier (owner
  decision Q11 sets minSdk 30). Android reports no audio times, so a result is placed by when it arrived (an utterance spans
  its first partial to its final), confidence is null when the recogniser gives none, and a recogniser that ends after each
  utterance is restarted by the rules above. Android pauses recording on audio-focus loss while this clock keeps running.
- Errors are `AppFailure`s the coordinator already understands: `microphonePermissionDenied`, `transcriptionUnavailable`,
  `audioSessionFailed` (with the cause).
- `TranscriptionService` (actor) becomes `TranscriptionSession` (class); `isTranscribing` is a property; times are epoch ms;
  `TranscriptDisplayItem` is in `ports/speech.ts`. `setOnTranscriptUpdate`, `setCurrentPhaseProvider` and
  `setRegionVocabulary` keep their names; `setOnError` is new.
- JS-only guards: a second start without a stop releases the first engine; a throwing store, phase provider or screen callback
  loses nothing but its own call (the segment is still tagged `preflight`, the line still shown); events of an engine that
  was stopped or replaced are dropped.

## Beyond the ticket

`src/adapters/coordinatorAudio.ts` is not in the A3 row of the port map. The carry-forward list asks for the
interruption-gap, error and phase-provider hooks of `AudioRecorderLike` and `TranscriptionServiceLike`; A2 built the recorder
as its own port, whose shape differs (`start`/`stop`/`audioLevel()` against `startRecording`/`stopRecording`/an `audioLevel`
property, typed gap reasons against the text the coordinator prints). Without a bridge I1 cannot build the coordinator. It
is 25 lines; delete it if I1 prefers to reshape the interface.

## Device-only checks for I3

1. iOS 26 device, en-US model installed: speak, see volatile lines, then final lines stored with plain-English confidence and
   times; the vocabulary corrections appear.
2. Recorder and analyzer at once: the audio file and the transcript are both complete; no audio-session errors.
3. Model not installed: the flight records without a transcript, `prepareSpeechAssets()` with network installs it, the next
   flight transcribes. iOS earlier than 26 reports unavailable.
4. Phone call and Siri mid-flight, with the app in the background and in the foreground: transcription resumes by itself.
   Plug and unplug headphones and a Bluetooth headset: transcription survives the route change.
5. Thirty minutes with the screen off (audio background mode): final segments keep arriving and are stored.
6. Replay: the highlighted transcript line follows the audio within about a second; measure the drift after 30 minutes and
   after an interruption.
7. Android 13+: transcription works in airplane mode with an on-device model installed, restarts after silence, and no audio
   leaves the phone. Android 12 and earlier: transcription is unavailable and the flight records.
8. Denied microphone and speech permissions on both platforms: recording and the track continue.
9. S2 (plan): word accuracy of this module against the Swift output on three recorded cockpit clips.
